cases:
  - id: release-readiness-review
    index: "01"
    engagement: Product & Engineering Review
    title: Turning a broad release concern into a decision-ready review.
    summary: An anonymised repository-based assessment for a small B2B software product preparing for broader use.
    situation: Questions about release readiness cut across runtime architecture, data handling, API security, testing, delivery and monitoring. Looking at any one area alone would have produced an incomplete answer.
    examined: I reviewed the repository, architecture, test suite, delivery configuration and technical documentation. Findings were traced back to concrete evidence and grouped by the decisions they affected.
    clearer: The useful distinction was not simply between good and bad implementation. It was between release blockers, risks that could be accepted temporarily, open questions requiring specialist input and improvements that could wait.
    changed: The review produced a prioritised assessment, a set of decision records and a walkthrough connecting the most important findings to recommended next steps.
    unchanged: Assessment remained separate from remediation. I did not change the product, validate production behaviour, certify specialist domains or take ownership of decisions that belonged with the client.
    outcome: The team received a decision-ready evidence base for release planning rather than an undifferentiated list of issues.
  - id: deliberate-quality-automation
    index: "02"
    engagement: Product & Engineering Advisory
    title: Making testing and AI-assisted development more deliberate.
    summary: An anonymised engagement with an established software team improving release confidence while testing practical uses of AI.
    situation: The team wanted to strengthen quality practices while experimenting with AI-assisted development. The risk was adding more tools without clarifying which checks should block delivery and which experiments should remain advisory.
    examined: Together we worked through testing layers, CI ordering, an AI code-review prototype, tracking and accessibility priorities, and repository-level instructions for AI-assisted changes.
    clearer: Fast, dependable feedback needed to come before broader automation. Unit tests could act as a blocking gate; slower checks and AI review needed different authority; some attractive automation was not yet worth adding.
    changed: The team put unit tests into regular use, kept AI code review non-blocking and selected tracking and accessibility as nearer-term quality priorities. Visual regression was deliberately deferred while layouts were still changing frequently.
    unchanged: We did not automate test writing, make AI feedback authoritative or describe prototypes as organisation-wide adoption.
    outcome: The work left a clearer sequence for quality improvements and explicit boundaries for where AI could assist without owning release decisions.
